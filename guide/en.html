    Step 1

    The principle behind the method

    Two minutes to understand what the ElHydro method does and what you need before you start.

    What is it?

    The ElHydro method consists of cropping the invisible or useless peripheral pixels in VR (tangential crop) in order to reinvest all the GPU power saved into targeted supersampling, right where the eye is looking. Thanks to a dedicated mathematical calculator, it compensates for the reduced field of view to preserve a perfect optical geometry while drastically increasing effective sharpness (PPD). The result is surgical visual clarity without relying on destructive third-party drivers, while locking in a maximum and stable framerate.

    In other words, and more simply: we cut off (crop) the useless edges of the image that you never look at in game, to relieve your graphics card. The power freed up is reinvested directly into the center of your vision, which makes the game much sharper while staying perfectly smooth.

    What you need to set up this method

    • OpenXR Toolkit – PrimaShock mod: the toolkit is what lets you perform the crop operations, among other things.
    • The Excel file for your headset (or ElHydro Calculator, which uses the same formulas): it gives the correct values we will need later.
    The thread running through this guide

    Choose a crop → calculate the matching resolution → enter the 6 crop values and the 2 resolution values in the Toolkit. Everything else is preparation and fine-tuning.

    Before installing the mod, make sure you have installed the original version 1.3.2 of OpenXR Toolkit.

    2. Run the installer and follow the instructions to complete the setup.

    Step B – Installing the PrimaShock mod

    Once the base toolkit is installed, follow these steps to apply the modified files:

    1. Download the files: download the mod DLL file via the ZIP download button. Unzip it and extract the file(s).
    2. Locate the installation folder: it is usually located at C:\Program Files\OpenXR-Toolkit.
    3. Back up your files: rename the existing file XR_APILAYER_MBUCCHIA_toolkit.dll to something like XR_APILAYER_MBUCCHIA_toolkit.back.
    4. Copy the new DLL: drag and drop the file XR_APILAYER_MBUCCHIA_toolkit.dll extracted from the PrimaShock ZIP into the installation folder. If you are using the 3D BOOST + PERFORMANCE CROP mod: also back up the original file, then copy the new shader file (postprocess.hlsl) into C:\Program Files\OpenXR-Toolkit\Shaders.
    5. Update the shaders: open the Shaders folder in the installation directory.
    6. Back up the original shaders: create a backup copy of the original shader files.
    7. Copy the new shaders: copy the contents of the shaders folder from the mod ZIP file into this directory.
    8. Check: open the OpenXR Toolkit companion app. It should now display “Primal Shock mod”.

    Step 5

    Choosing the crop

    How much field of view we remove at the top, bottom and sides — and why we think in degrees.

    The six values

    You now need to choose the crop values: Up (top), Down (bottom), Left/Left (left), Right/Right (right), as well as the Left/Right and Right/Left crop (the ones near the nose). ElHydro suggests values depending on the car type; you can pick one of these values and test it. These values are indicative, and you can choose the one that suits you best. Feel, visual preference, GPU, CPU and many other criteria mean there is no single value that could be considered the best.

    My personal values

    Personally I use these values, but again they depend on my own feel, the power of my PC, etc.: 70 or 75 Up, 90 or 95 Down and 99% on the sides. The crop near the nose (L/R and R/L) is calculated via the Excel file.

    Percentages vs degrees: what you need to understand

    The percentage (%) simply indicates the portion of the original view that you keep. For example, 70% means you keep 70% of the headset's native field of view and remove 30%.

    Degrees (°) measure the real angular aperture perceived by your eyes. This value in degrees is the one to keep in mind, because it is the only universal optical unit: each headset model has a different native field of view, and a 70% setting will not give the same angle on a Dream Air as on another headset.

    This is essential for what follows: in ElHydro's Excel sheet, you must enter the value in degrees (the number in brackets in the Toolkit, e.g. 31.20°) and not the percentage, so that the resolution formulas are accurate.

    Step 6

    Values in Excel

    Three angles in, one resolution out. This is where the method does its math.

    Open the Excel file

    Green: the 3 cells (Up, Dn, Left Left)

    These are the only values you need to edit manually in the spreadsheet. You must enter exactly the angles in degrees you have chosen (here: 31.20°, 40.10° and 54.50°). All the other values below (Left Right, Right Left, Right Right) are calculated automatically by the geometric formula: never edit them by hand.

    Cell%Angle to enter
    Up70%31.20
    Dn90%40.10
    Left Left (LL)99%54.50
    Left Right / Right Left / Right Right: calculated automatically, do not touch
    Make sure you enter degrees, not percentages!

    In the example above: for Up, type 31.20, not 70.

    Blue: the supersampling you choose

    110% SS row (5324 × 3220): this is the native clarity target (0.00% PPD-Eff difference). By applying this level, you mathematically compensate for the lost surface and get back exactly the same pixel density per degree as the uncropped headset.

    The higher levels (example: the 140% level at 6006 × 3633): as soon as you go above the 0.00% row, you enter pure supersampling (Super Sampling).

    To start, try the 2 values highlighted in yellow that will appear in your Excel file. For example, start with the 0.00% value (in this case 5324 × 3220) and go up step by step depending on your graphics card and your PC. For example, if I choose 160%, I will enter these values in the toolkit: 6421 × 3883.

    The values are different depending on the headset and the crop. Make sure you use the right Excel file and look at the right row once you have entered the 3 values (the picture above is for the Pimax Dream Air only).

    Step 7

    Toolkit configuration

    Before entering the crop, we set up the Performance tab. Don't worry: you will be able to test and adjust your values directly in game with the toolkit — you will see black bars if you apply a crop that is too aggressive, for example.

    Open the game and open the toolkit now

    1. Restart the game and the toolkit.
    2. Under Performance: set CAS with Size 100%. Set Sharpness according to the results you get in game (so test it: I sometimes set it to 0%, sometimes 20% and sometimes 75% depending on the game, so it is up to you).
    3. Set Turbo mode to ON.

    Then we move on to the part we are most interested in: the crop.

    Step 8

    Entering the crop and the resolution

    The decisive step: the 6 crop values and the 2 resolution values in the System tab.

    In the System tab

    1. Go to System.
    2. Enter the 6 crop values (Field of view → Advanced).
    3. Set “Override resolution” to YES.
    4. Enter the 2 values in “Override resolution” (width and height).
    5. Do not touch the values in the red box (whatever values are shown there).
    6. Close the toolkit via “Exit menu”.
    7. Restart the game and everything should be fine.
    NEVER click APPLY

    Do not touch the values in the red box (“Enter your native resolution” and “Apply resolution”). If you clicked Apply and confirmed by mistake, reset the toolkit again (step 4) and restart everything to be safe.

    Step 9

    A few notes and additional settings

    The settings around the method that make a difference on the image and the fps.

    Toolkit and Pimax Play

    • 3D boost: I leave it at 0 for my part (also recommended by ElHydro).
    • The SBOYS 3 drivers must absolutely be disabled (the distortion profiles do not match the values in the Excel file well).
    • In Pimax Play, I have quality set to 1.0.
    • Sun Glasses: ON (Light) — essential setting recommended by ElHydro on the Dream Air's OLED panels to remove the dull haze and open up the blacks. Test it yourself: on some games I prefer to disable it (Appearance → Post processing ON).
    • Vibrance: 15 to 25 — to compensate for the flat colorimetry and boost the contrast of the kerbs and the track (Appearance → Post processing ON).
    • Turn off Smart Smoothing in Pimax Play.

    The magic anti-aliasing combo: NVIDIA MFAA + MSAA 2x in game

    It is one of the secret pillars of the ElHydro method to get an ultra-sharp image without hurting performance:

    • In the NVIDIA Control Panel: go to Manage 3D settings → select your game (or the global settings) and set Multi-Frame Sampled AA (MFAA) to On.
    • In the game's graphics settings (e.g. AMS2): set anti-aliasing to MSAA 2x (Low/Medium) and make sure you disable Post-Processing Anti-Aliasing (Off).

    Why this setting? NVIDIA's MFAA hardware technology combines temporal samples to automatically turn MSAA 2x into rendering equivalent to MSAA 4x, while keeping the negligible performance cost of 2x. Disabling Post-Processing AA avoids applying a destructive software blur filter.

    You need to test

    Play with the size of the crop and the supersampling (values in Override resolution) to find the right balance, especially in terms of fps. I am giving you the process, but the values are often personal, depending on your own taste and your PC: results in fps or quality may vary.

    Step 10

    Adding the Helmet Overlay

    Optional: a realistic racing helmet overlaid on the image, controlled by Le Dour's OpenXR layer, on top of the PrimaShock crop.

    The principle

    This system adds a realistic helmet overlay inside VR, creating the feeling of actually looking through a racing helmet instead of simply seeing the game through the headset.

    This setup is built around the system developed by @elydro, which is based on PrimaShock. PrimaShock itself was created by another developer: @elydro adapted the PrimaShock system through his converter, which makes it possible to calculate the correct resolution corresponding to the desired FOV crop. The helmet itself is then added and controlled through Le Dour's OpenXR layer.

    1. Crop and resolution first

    Before installing the helmet overlay, your PrimaShock-based setup (steps 1 to 9) must already be installed and working correctly. For this helmet system, the recommended starting crop is:

    CropValue
    Top20%
    Bottom24.9%

    These values work very well as a general starting point and adapt nicely to many different helmet designs.

    Important: calculate the correct resolution

    When changing the crop to 20% top / 24.9% bottom, you also need to change the rendering resolution accordingly. Use @elydro's converter (the Excel file) to calculate the correct resolution for this crop, then enter it in OpenXR Toolkit as explained in steps 6 to 8. In ElHydro Calculator, the Helmet button applies this preset directly.

    • Set the crop to 20% top / 24.9% bottom.
    • Calculate the corresponding resolution (Excel or calculator).
    • Enter the calculated resolution into OpenXR Toolkit.
    • Then proceed with the overlay installation.

    2. Install Le Dour's OpenXR layer

    Download Le Dour's OpenXR FOV Crop / Helmet Overlay layer: Le Dour – latest GitHub release. The easiest method is to use the installer. The program itself is normally installed in:

    C:\Program Files\OpenXR-Layer-fov-crop\

    If you download the ZIP version instead, extract the files to a permanent folder and follow the included installation instructions. The configuration files to edit are located here (paste this path into the Windows Explorer address bar):

    %LOCALAPPDATA%\XR_APILAYER_MLEDOUR_fov_crop\

    The first time, start by editing settings.json. After you launch a game for the first time, Le Dour automatically creates a separate file for that game: every game can therefore have its own helmet configuration.

    3. Enable the system

    Open settings.json, or the specific <game>_settings.json file after it has been created.

    At the beginning of the file, and inside the helmet_overlay section, you must have:

    "enabled": true

    If either of these is set to false, the overlay will not work. For live editing, also add:

    "live_edit": true

    4. Set all Le Dour crop values to 0

    The crop is already handled by PrimaShock: we do not want Le Dour to apply a second crop on top of it. Every crop value in Le Dour's JSON must therefore be 0:

    "crop_left_percent": 0,
"crop_right_percent": 0,
"crop_top_percent": 0,
"crop_bottom_percent": 0
    Important

    If other numbers appear in these fields on a screenshot, ignore them: they were used during testing. For this setup, everything must be 0 so that the two cropping systems do not overlap.

    5. Adjusting the helmet

    The three main settings:

    "distance_m": 0.15,
"horizontal_fov_deg": 125,
"vertical_offset_deg": -14

    These values are extremely personal: it depends on your headset, your eyes, the helmet design, and how close you want the helmet to feel.

    • Distance — distance_m controls how close the helmet is. Lower = closer: 0.25 farther away, 0.15 much closer and more realistic, 0.10 / 0.09 / 0.08 extremely close. Below approximately 0.10, the cheek pads can almost overlap in the center of your vision.
    • Horizontal FOV — horizontal_fov_deg controls the apparent width. If the cheek pads are too close after reducing the distance, increase the FOV: this pushes the sides apart. But it also enlarges the visor opening vertically; distance and FOV are adjusted together.
    • Vertical offset — vertical_offset_deg moves the helmet: positive = up, negative = down. Adjust until the visor sits naturally around your eyes.

    The author's reference settings: distance 0.15, FOV 125, offset -14. Try them first, then fine-tune.

    6. Live editing and additional helmets

    With live_edit: true, you can leave the game running: change a number, save (Ctrl + S), and the change is applied in VR. Only changing the helmet image requires restarting the game.

    Le Dour includes only a few basic PNGs. Put your helmet PNGs in:

    %LOCALAPPDATA%\XR_APILAYER_MLEDOUR_fov_crop\helmets\

    Then, in the helmet_overlay section of the game's JSON, replace only the filename:

    "image": "Ferrari 488 GTE.png"

    The name must match the PNG exactly (spaces, capital letters, .png). The safest way is to copy and paste the filename.

    JSON warning

    Do not change the structure of the file: only true/false where instructed, the crop numbers, distance, FOV, offset and the PNG filename. A comma, quotation mark or bracket deleted by mistake can stop the system from working.
